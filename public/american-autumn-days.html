An American Autumn — Day by Day

Washington DC → Boston · Sun 27 Sept – Sat 10 Oct 2026 · ← back to the interactive map · lock it in →

The spine of the trip, in one line: you open among the institutions the Founders built, stand where Lincoln spoke, walk the mill that forged the postwar boom, sleep beside the New Deal, stand in the room where the postwar economic order was written, watch the politics of what came after at a county fair — and finish where the Revolution began, at a hockey game, with a flight home. Each day below carries the plan, why it’s shaped this way (the decisions), and what else is in reach (the bench).

Washington DC · 4 nights ✓ Hilton Garden Inn Downtown, 815 14th St NW — booked

1Arrive — and write the day offSun 27 Sept
  • Land, one train to the hotel — Silver line direct from Dulles, Blue direct from National; McPherson Square is across the street
  • The Mall at dusk only if the legs allow: Lincoln Memorial → reflecting pool → Washington Monument
  • Dinner near the hotel. Nothing booked, nothing scheduled
Why it’s like thisDeliberately empty — scheduling against jetlag ruins day two as well. The Nationals’ season finale (Nats–Mets, the last game of the MLB regular season) is on this afternoon a Metro ride away, and we cut it for exactly this reason.
2Capitol Hill — three branches in 800 yardsMon 28 Sept
  • Capitol tour (booked ahead), then the Library of Congress — the Jefferson Building is the most beautiful interior in the city; give the reading-room gallery and the Gutenberg Bible ninety minutes, not twenty
  • Supreme Court, two minutes’ walk — courtroom lecture when out of session
  • 10 minutes direct on the Metro from the hotel (McPherson Sq → Capitol South)
Why it’s like thisThe legislative day is the trip’s thesis statement, so it goes first while you’re freshest.
Also in reachAn evening gig at the 9:30 Club or The Anthem — the only DC nights left after this are museum-tired ones, so if there’s a listing you like, tonight is the night.
3The founding documentsTue 29 Sept
  • National Archives at opening, before the queue — the Declaration, the Constitution, the Bill of Rights in the rotunda
  • Afternoon: American History museum — straight to the Presidency and Democracy halls and the Star-Spangled Banner
  • Lafayette Square and the White House view on the walk home (6 minutes from the hotel)
  • After dark: Jefferson & FDR memorials — taxi round the Tidal Basin; both are best at night and empty then
Why it’s like thisSeeing the actual engrossed Constitution is the heart of the whole trip’s theme — everything else in DC is commentary on it. Two museums in a day is the ceiling; we don’t try to “do the Smithsonian.”
4ArlingtonWed 30 Sept
  • Arlington National Cemetery — the Changing of the Guard at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, the Kennedy graves, Arlington House and its view back over the river. Blue line direct from the hotel’s stop
  • Afternoon stays loose — Ford’s Theatre is 8 minutes from the hotel if you want the Lincoln bookend
  • Early night: tomorrow starts at 07:30
Why it’s like thisChosen over a fourth museum day. After three days of marble and exhibition halls the returns collapse — Arlington changes the register: outdoors, solemn, and still entirely on-theme. This day exists at all because we traded the Rangers’ home opener for a fourth DC night.
Also in reachMount Vernon (Washington’s estate, 30 min south) was the other change-of-register option · Great Falls Park (30 min) for something genuinely wild · the Holocaust Museum or NMAAHC if you’d rather have the heavy museum after all — both need timed passes.

The long Thursday · DC → the Hudson

5Gettysburg, the steel, the GapThu 1 Oct~305 mi · 5h 35m
07:30Collect the rental from a downtown agency (one-way DC→Boston) and drive north
09:45–14:00Gettysburg — the film and Cyclorama, then the battlefield auto tour: Little Round Top, the High Water Mark, the Soldiers’ National Cemetery where Lincoln spoke the Address you read in marble on day one
15:50–16:50Bethlehem SteelStacks — the Hoover-Mason Trestle, 2,000 ft of elevated walkway along the preserved blast furnaces of America’s second-largest steelmaker. Then a drink in the ArtsQuest glass atrium facing the stacks
~17:30Delaware Water Gap overlook — golden hour; sunset 18:43
~19:00Bear Mountain Inn — Overlook Lodge ✓ booked — straight into dinner at Restaurant 1915 (the Thursday kitchen closes at 20:00, the day’s real deadline), check in after, Hessian Lake if any light survives
Why it’s like thisGettysburg gets morning light and empty roads. The night moved from the Cold Spring draft to Bear Mountain when the booking was made — same west bank as the Academy, so Friday loses its bridge crossing, and the 1915 lodge throws in the first mile of the Appalachian Trail ever cut. Bethlehem is the answer to “where’s the peak post-war boom?” — New England’s mills are the wrong era (they died during the boom), but steel was the boom, and the mill that put the beams in the Golden Gate Bridge sits directly on this road. It pairs deliberately with Bretton Woods on Monday: the rules were written there, cashed in here. If the day runs late, the Gap drops first — this trip runs on political economy, not scenery. You also drive through three battleground House districts today (PA-10, PA-7, NJ-7): expect yard signs, not rallies.
Also in reachA licensed battlefield guide who rides in your car (book ahead — the splurge-worthy way to do Gettysburg) · a Steelwalker tour of the furnaces led by former steelworkers runs Thursdays — if a late-afternoon slot exists, it beats everything else on the campus · the Smithsonian-affiliated industrial museum at the stacks’ base closes 17:00, so it’s a sprint at best.
✓ Book: rental car (07:30 downtown pickup) · Gettysburg in-car guide · a 19:15 table at Restaurant 1915 (OpenTable / 845-786-2731 x1915) — the bed itself is ✓ booked.

The Hudson & the stadium

6West Point, Springwood, Zach BryanFri 2 Oct~200 mi · 3h 45m
09:10Leave Bear Mountain — the Academy gate is twelve minutes up 9W, no bridge
09:30Security at the Highland Falls Visitor Center (photo ID)
10:00–11:30West Point tour — the oldest continuously occupied military post in America; Trophy Point’s Hudson view in autumn
11:45North over the Mid-Hudson Bridge, 45 minutes
12:30Hyde Park — tickets at the Visitor Center for the next Springwood tour (same-day, first-come, no cash). The house FDR was born in, lived in all his life, and is buried behind, in the rose garden
~14:30East — through Hartford ahead of the Friday rush
~17:15Milford check-in en route — Holiday Inn Express ✓ booked, bags in the room, twenty minutes from the stadium
~18:00Gillette Stadium — parked, fed and in before the openers. Tickets ✓ booked
19:00–lateGabriella Rose → Gregory Alan Isakov → ZACH BRYAN ~20:30. Out south against the Boston-bound crowd, I-495 north, bed by ~23:45
Why it’s like thisThis order is the third draft, and the best one. FDR-first forced a race against a security cutoff and made Springwood a lottery — you had to win the first tour slot of the day. Academy-first turns the mansion into a plan: any early-afternoon slot works. It’s one thing at Hyde Park by choice — the house over the Library, born-lived-buried there — and the fallback is built in: if the slot offered is 14:00 or later, walk the grounds to the graves (free) and do the Library (no ticket) instead. FDR’s administration convened Bretton Woods; you’ll stand in the Gold Room on Monday. The bed slid from Providence to Milford at booking time: zero backtrack — post-show it’s 25 minutes with every mile pointing at Saturday.
Also in reachThe FDR Presidential Library — the first ever built, he designed it himself — if the tour times gift you a gap · tailgating properly if you skip the Milford check-in stop (the Holiday Inn Express desk runs all night — the escape hatch for exactly this call).
✓ Book: West Point 10:00 tour — the keystone, and now the last unbooked piece of this day. Tickets ✓ and the Milford bed ✓ are done.

New Hampshire · the mountains

7Inland to the NotchSat 3 Oct~165 mi · 3h 15m
  • ~09:00 north — Red Arrow Diner in Manchester (~10:00) if breakfast calls, and two minutes of the mile-long red-brick Amoskeag Millyard from the car
  • 11:40–14:00 Castle in the Clouds — the 1913 Lucknow mansion, lunch on the terrace above Lake Winnipesaukee
  • ~15:20 into North Woodstock — Woodstock Inn Brewery ✓ booked, the two-night base
  • Artist’s Bluff for the last light — 45 minutes, steep but short, a huge view straight down Franconia Notch. Sunset 18:22
  • Dinner downstairs — you are sleeping above your own brewpub
Why it’s like thisInland beats the coast: I-93 is the better road, marginally shorter, and it passes the castle. Amoskeag gets a drive-by and an honest label — its boom was Gilded Age, dead by 1935; the wrong era for our post-war thread, but a striking two minutes. We sleep in North Woodstock, not at the Omni: the grand hotel is Monday’s visit, not the bed — it’s eye-watering, and you only need an hour of it.
Also in reachPortsmouth NH as an alternative lunch stop if you’d rather a colonial seaport than a mansion (coast road instead of I-93 — but it forfeits the castle).
✓ Book: nothing new tonight — but tomorrow’s Cog (10:00) and Monday’s Mountaineer (13:30) must already be in hand.
8The Cog, the Gold Room & the lakeSun 4 Oct~60 mi out-and-back + the mountain
08:00Big breakfast at the inn — earned in advance: today is the summit
08:25Away north — the base station is 45 minutes and wants you there by 09:15
10:00Mount Washington Cog Railway to the summit of the Northeast — just under 3h round trip, an hour on top. Pack coat, hat and gloves: the summit averages 2°C with 35 mph wind in October, and is in cloud most days
13:15–14:30The Gold Room, Mount Washington Hotel — where 44 nations signed the IMF and World Bank into existence on 22 July 1944 — taken over lunch or a drink on the veranda facing the Presidentials. A visit, not a stay
15:15–17:45Back south 40 min to the Notch: Lonesome Lake — 3.2 miles, 2h30 round, cocoa at the AMC hut, Franconia Ridge reflected in the water. Sunset 18:22 — headtorches in the daypack as insurance
19:00Dinner downstairs at the brewery. You will have earned it twice
Why it’s like thisYour reshape, and it’s the best single day of the mountains: summit in the morning, treaty room for lunch, alpine lake in the afternoon. It only works because breakfast-to-base is 45 minutes and the Omni sits ten minutes from the railway. Two honest risks: weather (the summit is in cloud most days — morning departures get the better odds, and the Auto Road is the fallback if the Cog itself falls through) and daylight (the lake walk ends 35 minutes before sunset — start it by 15:30 or swap it for the Basin). Flume Gorge and the Kanc paid for the reshape: the Kanc survives as Monday morning’s drive, the Flume goes to the bench.
Also in reachFlume Gorge’s boardwalk ($18 timed) or the free Basin if the lake feels far after the summit · Loon Mountain gondola if legs mutiny (Cannon’s tramway is closed all 2026) · The Frost Place’s porch and poetry trail · rain: Lost River Gorge boulder caves, or Littleton’s candy counter and the brewery in the 18th-century mill.
✓ Book: the Cog — re-dated to Sunday 4 Oct, ~10:00, biodiesel — still the #1 item on the whole list.
9The Kanc by car, Crawford Notch by railMon 5 Oct~45 mi + the train
09:30–11:45The Kancamagus Highway east from Lincoln with the stops — Hancock Overlook, Sabbaday Falls, Rocky Gorge in the morning light. Fuel up first; no petrol the whole way
~12:00Lunch in North Conway — Moat Mountain Smokehouse if the queue is kind — and Zeb’s General Store two doors along; the outlets and NH’s no-sales-tax for any gear on the shopping list
13:30–17:45The Mountaineer — Conway Scenic’s 4–4.5h round trip from the 1874 station through Crawford Notch: Frankenstein Trestle, Willey Brook, the great New England mountain-railroad journey. Dome car if you can get it
~18:15Fryeburg — The Oxford House Inn ✓ booked, fifteen minutes’ walk from the fair gate. The Inn’s dining room is dark Mon–Tue, so the fair is dinner — and the midway under the lights is there if legs allow
Why it’s like thisTwo trains in one trip is not a bug. The Cog is a mountain-climbing machine; the Mountaineer is a landscape train — and Crawford Notch’s best views (Frankenstein Trestle, the Willey Brook crossings) belong to the railway anyway, so the old plan’s 302 roadside stops were always the consolation version. The car does the Kanc in the morning, the train does the Notch in the afternoon: both great corridors, neither done twice. The bed sits eight miles on at the Oxford House, inside fair week — you walk in past the parking queues, tonight and tomorrow.
Also in reachDiana’s Baths (1.2 mi, easy) fits before the train · the 09:00 Mountaineer instead, if you’d rather an early train and a loose afternoon (then Mount Willard, 1h30 from Crawford Depot, re-enters by car) · the Wildcat gondola for the best view of Mount Washington.
✓ Book: the Mountaineer, 13:30 Mon 5 Oct — dome class first choice (conwayscenic.com); foliage-week trains sell out well ahead. (Bed ✓ Oxford House booked.)

Maine · the fair, the coast, the campaign

10Fryeburg Fair & ME-2Tue 6 Oct~50 mi · 1h
  • Wake up inside fair week — breakfast at the Inn, then walk in as the barns open. No parking fields for you
  • 09:00–13:30 the Fryeburg Fair, round two — the ox and horse pulling, the livestock judging, harness racing, the midway in daylight. Work the political booths
  • ~15:00 into Portland — the Old Port and the working waterfront for the evening
Why it’s like thisThe fair fell into our lap — it runs 4–11 October and the road to Portland goes through Fryeburg anyway. Then it turned out to be the sharpest political stop on the route: Fryeburg sits in ME-2, the only Democratic-held House seat on defence in the whole Northeast — open (Golden retired), with Paul LePage running for the Republicans, four weeks before the vote, at an event 300,000 people attend. This is the retail politics the trip was originally built to find, and it costs nothing. The Senate race (Collins vs Platner) and an open Governor’s seat are live statewide too. The Oxford House booking upgraded the whole thing from a drive-through morning to a night-and-a-morning residency.
Also in reachJackson’s covered bridge and falls, 25 min back north, if you want one last White Mountains postcard before Maine.
✓ Book: Portland ×3.
11Portland — lighthouse, food, Bean boots at midnightWed 7 Oct
  • Morning: Portland Head Light at Cape Elizabeth — the classic Maine lighthouse-and-headland two hours
  • The city and its food the rest of the day — Eventide, Fore Street, Allagash; a genuinely excellent eating and brewing town, and a day built on that is a legitimate plan
  • Whatever the campaign throws up — check the Portland Press Herald, Maine Public and candidate socials this week
  • Evening: L.L.Bean Freeport, 25 minutes north — open 24 hours a day since 1951, no locks on the doors, and the rebuilt flagship campus opens this very autumn. Bean Boots (useful: the fair was muddy) and a monogrammed Boat and Tote
Why it’s like thisGoing to L.L.Bean at eleven at night is a better story than going at two in the afternoon. The campaign leg is deliberately loose — events publish a week or two out, so slack is the strategy, not a gap in it.
Also in reachKennebunkport and the Bushes’ Walker’s Point, 40 min south, if you want more coast today.
12The Maine day tripThu 8 Oct
  • Recommended — the coast: Boothbay Harbour (1h20) or Camden with the Mount Battie summit over Penobscot Bay (2h). Materially prettier water than Portland’s own
  • Or inland: Sabbathday Lake Shaker Village, 40 minutes — the last active Shaker community in the world
Why it’s like thisThis was once the emptiest day of the whole trip — a placeholder that read “more coast, or more politics.” It now carries committed options; after three mountain days, the coast is the recommended contrast. The third Portland night that makes this possible was a deliberate trade (we gave back a White Mountains night for it).
Also in reach◇ Maybe: if the Red Sox reach the ALDS they host Game 3 tonight — Fenway is 1h50 away. Known only after the wild-card round ends 1 Oct · the Bruins also host Utah tonight (a 3h40 round trip we considered and declined — the same team plays two days later, a 15-minute walk from the last hotel).

Massachusetts · the finish

13Salem, then BostonFri 9 Oct~105 mi · 2h 10m
  • Leave Portland by 10:30 — the whole day hinges on this one departure
  • 12:05–15:15 Salem — Haunted Happenings city-wide all month, the old town, Derby Wharf, the House of the Seven Gables, in the city’s 400th-anniversary year
  • ~16:00 Boston — check in at the Royal Sonesta ✓ booked (on the Charles in East Cambridge, by the Museum of Science) and drop the car for good; it’s a walking and subway city from here
  • Before dark (sunset 18:12): the downtown half of the Freedom Trail — the Common, State House, Old State House and Massacre site, Faneuil Hall
  • Evening: Boston Public Library, the Gardner Museum, or a gig (House of Blues, Roadrunner, Symphony Hall)
Why it’s like thisSalem is directly on the Portland–Boston road and October is its month — but it only fits alongside the Freedom Trail if you hold the 10:30 rule. Leave at 13:00 and Salem eats the afternoon, pushing the whole Trail into Saturday morning where it cannot fit. Friday crowds are busy but survivable; the crushes are weekends and Halloween. The Trail is split across two days on purpose.
Also in reach◇ Maybe: Red Sox ALDS Game 4 at Fenway tonight, if it exists · the Topsfield Fair (America’s oldest, 1818) is 15 minutes from Salem — though after Fryeburg it may be one fair too many.
✓ Book: bed ✓ Royal Sonesta booked — ask them to hold the bags on Saturday. Still open: the Friday-evening pick.

Considered & cut — the decision graveyard

Dartmouth Homecoming (the original centrepiece)
Homecoming runs 2–3 Oct, so its Friday-night bonfire clashed head-on with Zach Bryan — and the game itself is FCS-tier. Cut rather than kept as the lesser half of a tradition.
All college football
Everything reachable on the free Saturday (UNH, Harvard) is the same tier as the Dartmouth game already rejected; BC and Army were away. No point chasing a lesser version of the thing we dropped.
Rangers home opener at MSG (1 Oct)
Traded for the fourth DC night — the Gettysburg drive lands at 19:30, quarter of an hour after face-off. The Yankee Stadium wild-card maybe died with it.
Harrisburg & the PA State Capitol
Skipped to buy a proper Hudson stop instead of a motel night.
Rhinebeck / the FDR-first Friday
Two drafts of Friday died: the FDR-first version bet the day on winning the first mansion-tour slot; the 11:30-squeeze version quietly deleted the house interior. Academy-first fixed both.
Sleeping at the Omni Mount Washington
Too dear. The Gold Room and the veranda are the point, and a lunch buys them.
Woodstock VT & the Vermont day
Casualties of trading a White Mountains night to Maine; Woodstock survives only as a name on the swap bench.
Acadia
Twelve hours of driving for colour that turns a week or two later than the mountains. The Whites are the better show and the shorter road.
The Nationals’ season finale
On the arrival afternoon — cut when Sunday was declared a write-off. Correctly.
Cold Spring as the Thursday bed
A lovely draft that needed a bridge crossing before a 09:30 security cutoff. Bear Mountain won at booking time on the Academy’s own bank — and threw in Hessian Lake and the first mile of the Appalachian Trail.
Providence as the concert-night bed
Held the recommendation for weeks on the strength of a 1am diner, then lost to Milford on pure geometry: zero backtrack, every post-show mile pointing at Saturday. The Holiday Inn Express won’t be a story; the extra half-hour of Saturday will.
The North Conway night
Moved eight miles east to the Oxford House at booking time — same position for Tuesday, a proper 1913 inn, and you wake up inside the Fryeburg Fair instead of driving to it.
Flume Gorge & the four-part Sunday
The reshaped Sunday spent its morning on the Cog and its afternoon at the lake, so the boardwalk went to the bench and Mount Willard with it. The Kanc survived by becoming Monday morning’s drive — and Crawford Notch got upgraded from windscreen to dome car.
